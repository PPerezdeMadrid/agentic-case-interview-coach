Solventus Energy

Prompt #1

A Spanish renewable energy company has seen its EBITDA fall 34% over the past two years, despite total revenues growing 18%. The company generates electricity from onshore wind farms and recently expanded into two new businesses: residential electricity retail (B2C) and large-scale battery storage construction. The CEO has asked you to identify what is driving the profit decline and recommend a path forward.


The company operates across three business units: wind generation, B2C retail, and battery storage.
Revenue growth has been entirely driven by the two new units.
The CFO resigned three weeks ago.



Interviewer Guidance — Prompt #1

High-level plan of attack:


Revenue is growing, so the problem is unlikely to be top-line across the board — focus on the cost structure and margin by business unit.
The candidate should recognize that blended revenue growth can mask unit-level losses.
Push the candidate to separate the P&L by business unit before drawing conclusions.


Lay out your thoughts:


Use the profitability framework: Profit = Revenue − Costs.
Since total revenue is up, guide the candidate toward costs and margin mix.
Ask the candidate to hypothesize which of the three units might be dragging profitability and why.



Expected Analysis — Prompt #1

Dig deeper — gather facts:


Are all three business units profitable? No. Wind generation is highly profitable. The other two are not.
What are the margins by unit?

Wind generation: 41% EBITDA margin.
B2C retail: −8% EBITDA margin.
Battery storage: not yet generating revenue; CAPEX is running 3× the original budget.



Why is B2C retail losing money? The company entered a highly competitive market without a differentiated product or brand. Customer acquisition costs are high and annual churn is 28%, meaning the company is spending heavily to replace customers it loses.
Why is battery storage bleeding cash? Construction has been delayed by 22 months due to permitting issues and supply chain problems. Fixed costs and financing costs are accumulating with zero revenue offset.
Is the wind business being affected? Not operationally — but it is effectively cross-subsidizing the losses in the other two units, which is masking the severity of the problem.
Is management structured to run all three businesses? No. The leadership team has deep expertise in power generation but limited experience in retail or infrastructure project management.


Key insight: The company is not facing a single profitability problem — it is facing three separate business situations bundled into one P&L. The wind business is healthy. The retail business has a structural unit economics problem. The battery business is a capital project in distress.


Key Findings


Wind generation remains the core profit engine with strong margins; it is not the source of the problem.
B2C retail has negative margins driven by high churn and elevated customer acquisition costs; the business is destroying value at current scale.
Battery storage is a distressed capital project with no near-term revenue and costs accelerating beyond budget.
The blended P&L has obscured how serious the situation is in each new unit.



Final Recommendation

Based on your analysis, what recommendation would you give the client?

Interviewer guidance:


The candidate should recommend separating the financial reporting of each unit immediately to enable proper management oversight.
For B2C retail: either fix unit economics (pricing, churn reduction) or exit the business before losses compound further.
For battery storage: conduct an independent project review to determine whether to restructure, bring in a specialist partner, or write down the investment.
Protect and reinvest in the wind generation business, which is the only value-creating unit.
