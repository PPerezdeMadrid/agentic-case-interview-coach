Verdex AI

Prompt #1

A Spanish agri-tech startup has developed an AI-powered crop disease detection system for olive groves and vineyard farms. The system combines field sensors with computer vision models and alerts farmers 72 hours before disease outbreaks. Early traction has been especially strong in places like Candeleda, Talavera de la Reina, the Sierra de Gredos corridor, and parts of Extremadura and Alentejo. The company has grown annual recurring revenue at 60% per year for the past two years and now serves 340 agricultural cooperative clients across Spain and Portugal. Despite strong revenue growth, losses have widened each quarter and the burn rate has accelerated in the last two periods. The founding team has brought you in to identify the source of the profitability problem and advise on a path to financial sustainability.


The company charges clients an annual fee per hectare monitored.
Revenue is recurring and growing rapidly.
The company has approximately 14 months of runway remaining.



Interviewer Guidance — Prompt #1

High-level plan of attack:


Revenue is growing fast, so the problem is on the cost side — but the candidate must identify which costs and why they scale poorly.
This is a unit economics case. Guide the candidate to think about what it costs to serve one additional customer versus what that customer pays.
Strong candidates will distinguish between the cost of acquiring a customer and the cost of serving them over time.


Lay out your thoughts:


Use the profitability framework.
Since revenue is clearly growing, focus the analysis on gross margin and the cost structure beneath it.
Ask the candidate to hypothesize why margins might be thin despite a software-like product.



Expected Analysis — Prompt #1

Dig deeper — gather facts:


What is the current gross margin? Approximately 18% — very low for a technology company, which would typically target 60–80%.
Why is gross margin so low if the product is AI software? Because delivering the product requires installing and maintaining physical sensors in the field. Hardware costs, field technician visits, and sensor replacements make each customer expensive to serve.
What does the company charge per hectare? The pricing was deliberately set low to accelerate market penetration among price-sensitive agricultural cooperatives, especially smaller olive and vineyard groups in places like Candeleda and inland Castilla-La Mancha.
What does it cost to acquire a new customer? Customer acquisition costs are high due to lengthy sales cycles with cooperative decision-making bodies. The payback period on CAC is currently 38 months.
Are all customers equally profitable? No. Larger cooperatives with more hectares under management are significantly more profitable per account because fixed onboarding and installation costs are spread across more billable hectares. Small cooperatives are loss-making on a per-account basis.
Is there a version of the product that doesn't require hardware? The team is developing a satellite-based software-only model that would eliminate field sensor costs entirely — but it requires 9 months of additional development and investment the company may not be able to fund at current burn.
What is driving the acceleration in burn? The company has been signing new customers aggressively, which front-loads hardware and installation costs before any recurring revenue is collected.


Key insight: The company is caught in a structural trap: its growth model requires spending cash upfront on hardware to onboard customers who generate thin margins over a long payback period. Growing faster makes the losses worse, not better, unless the unit economics are fixed first.


Key Findings


Gross margin of 18% reflects a business that is hardware-dependent despite being positioned as a software company.
Per-customer economics are negative for small cooperative clients, which represent a significant portion of the base.
CAC payback of 38 months means the company is funding future returns with today's cash — unsustainable at 14 months of runway.
The satellite software model would structurally fix the margin problem but requires capital the company may not have.

Creative Question

Assume the founders agree Verdex must improve economics within the next 6 months, but they do not want to fully stop growth. What are two or three practical initiatives you would propose to improve near-term unit economics and cash burn while preserving the most attractive customers?

Interviewer guidance:

Strong answers should propose practical, case-specific ideas such as:

- Segment customers and stop selling to small, loss-making cooperatives.
- Introduce separate hardware, installation, or maintenance fees instead of bundling everything into one low recurring price.
- Offer minimum-acreage thresholds or multi-year contracts for smaller customers.
- Shift sales effort toward larger cooperatives with better payback economics.
- Pilot a lighter-touch service model that reduces field visits and replacement costs.
- Concentrate commercial effort on denser farming clusters such as Candeleda, Talavera de la Reina, and other areas where technician routes can be served more efficiently.

Weak answers will stay generic, ignore the runway constraint, or propose ideas that require large upfront investment before cash burn improves.



Final Recommendation

Based on your analysis, what recommendation would you give the client?

Interviewer guidance:


The candidate should recommend an immediate halt to unprofitable customer segments (small cooperatives) and a focus on large accounts with better unit economics.
Raise prices or restructure contracts to recover hardware and installation costs upfront or via a separate equipment fee.
Prioritize and accelerate the software-only model as the strategic fix — explore bridge financing or a strategic partnership to fund the 9-month development gap.
Strong candidates will flag the runway constraint explicitly and note that operational changes must generate cash savings within 6 months to avoid a funding crisis.
