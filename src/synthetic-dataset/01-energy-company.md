# Solventus Energy

## Prompt #1

Your client is Solventus Energy, a Spanish renewable energy company operating three business units: onshore wind generation, residential electricity retail (B2C), and large-scale battery storage construction. Over the past two years, group EBITDA has fallen 34% even though total revenue has grown 18%. The CEO has brought you in to find out what is driving the profit decline and to recommend a path forward.

# Interviewer Guidance

## Case type

Profitability case where the diagnostic unit is the business line, not the company as a whole. Blended P&L trends can hide unit-level value destruction, so segmentation is the critical first move rather than a standard price/volume breakdown.

## Expected opening from a strong candidate

- Recaps the situation in their own words: group EBITDA down 34% over two years while revenue is up 18%, across three business lines.
- Clarifies the objective before analyzing anything: is the CEO looking for a diagnosis only, a turnaround plan, or both, and is there a time horizon in mind?
- Asks whether performance can be viewed by business unit rather than only at the consolidated level.
- Lays out an initial structure before touching any numbers, e.g. "I would like to look at profitability unit by unit before drawing conclusions about the group as a whole."

## Working hypothesis a strong candidate should form early

Since revenue is growing but EBITDA is falling, the problem is unlikely to be broad-based top-line weakness. The more likely story is that one or both of the newer, revenue-growing units are unprofitable and are dragging down group EBITDA even as they add to the top line.

## Facts to provide upon request

Company structure:

- The company operates three business units: onshore wind generation, residential (B2C) electricity retail, and large-scale battery storage construction.
- Revenue growth over the past two years has come entirely from the two newer units; wind generation revenue has been broadly flat.

Organizational context:

- The CFO resigned three weeks ago.
- Management has deep expertise in power generation but comparatively limited experience running a retail business or a large infrastructure project.

## If the candidate stalls or keeps repeating the same framing

Ask directly: "Would it help to look at margins separately for each of the three business units?" That should be enough to unlock the segmentation insight if the candidate has not proposed it already.

# Expected Analysis

## Key insight (lead with this)

Solventus does not have one company-wide profitability problem. It has three different business situations bundled into a single P&L, and the healthy one is subsidizing the other two.

## Supporting evidence

- Wind generation, at a 41% EBITDA margin, is the only proven profit engine and is not the source of the decline.
- B2C retail has structurally negative unit economics: a -8% margin combined with 28% annual churn means the company is spending heavily to acquire customers it cannot keep, destroying value with every incremental customer under the current model.
- Battery storage is a distressed capital project, not an operating loss: zero revenue, 3x CAPEX, and a 22-month delay mean fixed and financing costs are accumulating with nothing yet to offset them.
- Because revenue growth came entirely from the two loss-making units, the consolidated top line looks healthy even as consolidated EBITDA deteriorates. The blended view is actively hiding how severe the problem is.

## What this means for the client

The fix is not a company-wide cost program. It requires unit-specific decisions: protect wind, decide fast on retail, and separately decide whether to rescue, partner with someone, or write down battery storage.

## Business unit facts

- Wind generation: 41% EBITDA margin. Operationally healthy, no reported issues.
- B2C retail: -8% EBITDA margin. Annual customer churn is 28%.
- Battery storage: no revenue yet. CAPEX is running at 3x the original budget, and the project is delayed by 22 months.

## Why each unit looks the way it does

- B2C retail entered a highly competitive residential market without a differentiated product or brand. Customer acquisition costs are high, and losing more than a quarter of the customer base every year means the company is constantly paying to replace churned customers instead of growing net share.
- Battery storage has been slowed by permitting issues and supply chain problems, pushing the timeline out 22 months. Fixed costs and financing costs on the project keep accruing with no revenue to offset them.
- Wind generation is not affected operationally by the other two units, but its healthy margin is effectively cross-subsidizing their losses, which masks how serious the retail and battery situations are at the consolidated level.

## Organizational capability

Management's strongest capability is power generation; retail and large infrastructure project execution are comparatively new and weaker competencies for the team. The CFO resigned three weeks ago, which may be a signal of underlying financial pressure but is not itself a driver of the EBITDA decline.

# Final Recommendation

Based on your analysis, what recommendation would you give the CEO?

## Recommendation

Stop managing Solventus as one blended P&L. Separate reporting and decision-making by business unit immediately, protect and reinvest in wind generation, and make a fast, explicit call on B2C retail and battery storage rather than letting them keep drawing on wind's cash flow.

## Supporting evidence

- Wind generation's 41% EBITDA margin funds the group and should not be further diluted by cross-subsidizing the other two units.
- B2C retail's -8% margin and 28% churn show the unit is destroying value at current scale, so it needs a fast fix (pricing, churn reduction) or an exit before losses compound further.
- Battery storage's zero revenue, 3x CAPEX, and 22-month delay mean it is a distressed project, not a scaling business, and needs an independent review rather than the same management approach that built the wind business.

## Risks and limitations

- Exiting B2C retail may create reputational and strategic consequences beyond the immediate P&L.
- Restructuring or writing down battery storage means acknowledging sunk-cost losses, which can be organizationally difficult, especially without a permanent CFO in place.
- Management's strength in generation does not automatically transfer to fixing retail or infrastructure execution problems, so a capability gap may need to be addressed alongside the financial fix.

## Next steps

- Stand up unit-level P&Ls immediately so future decisions are made on segmented, not blended, data.
- Set a short, explicit deadline for B2C retail to show improved unit economics before deciding whether to exit.
- Launch an independent technical and financial review of the battery storage project to decide between restructuring, bringing in a specialist partner, or writing down the investment.
