Calibration reference: worked examples of scores 1, 2, 3, and 4 for each interaction-quality dimension, built around a case that does not appear in any golden set (a B2B SaaS company selling inventory-management software to retailers; ARR up 70% annually for two years, losses widening each quarter, 11-month runway, 210 active accounts, CAC payback 22 months, gross margin 64%). Anchor your scoring to these, not to how fluent or agreeable the candidate sounds — a well-written turn can still repeat itself, ignore what was asked, invent a fact, over- or under-claim confidence, or contradict an earlier turn. Score what actually happened turn by turn, not the overall impression the transcript leaves.

## groundedness

Score 1: Candidate: "From what I've seen with similar SaaS companies, their CAC is probably around $2,000 and LTV is maybe $15,000" — no such figures appear anywhere in the case data or transcript, and the candidate presents them as recalled facts rather than assumptions.
Score: 1 — states facts not provided in the case and treats them as given.

Score 2: Candidate correctly uses the 64% gross margin from the case, but also says "referencing typical benchmarks for this segment, sales cycles usually run about six months" and "churn is likely under 5% annually for this kind of contract" — neither figure appears anywhere in the case data, and both are delivered as recalled industry knowledge rather than flagged guesses.
Score: 2 — mixes a real case figure with two invented, unflagged figures presented as fact — more than the single blurred assumption of a 3, but not wholesale fabrication like a 1.

Score 3: Candidate correctly uses the 64% gross margin and 22-month CAC payback from the case and flags one assumption explicitly ("assuming the self-serve rollout keeps the same growth rate"), but later uses a specific "roughly 25% margin improvement" figure without flagging it as an assumption.

Score 4: Every number the candidate uses (64% margin, 22-month payback, 11-month runway, 210 accounts) is one already given in the case, and every time the candidate reasons beyond the data ("I'll assume large retail chains average at least twice the store count of independent retailers") it is explicitly flagged as an assumption.
Score: 4 — nearly every claim traceable to a case fact, assumptions clearly labeled.
