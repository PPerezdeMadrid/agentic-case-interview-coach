Calibration reference: worked examples of scores 1, 2/3, and 4 for each interaction-quality dimension. Anchor your scoring to these, not to how fluent or agreeable the candidate sounds — a well-written turn can still repeat itself, ignore what was asked, invent a fact, over- or under-claim confidence, or contradict an earlier turn. Score what actually happened turn by turn, not the overall impression the transcript leaves.

## clarity_and_concision

Score 1: Candidate restates "so losses are widening despite revenue growth" at the start of three separate turns without adding new content each time.
Score: 1 — repeats the same idea in nearly every turn instead of stating it once.

Score 3: Candidate mostly states each point once, but repeats the "38-month CAC payback" figure in a later turn when it was not asked for.
Score: 3 — one unprompted repetition of a key number.

Score 4: Across the whole transcript the CAC-payback number, the gross-margin number, and the final recommendation are each stated exactly once; a later turn references one of them ("as I mentioned, the 38-month payback...") without restating the number.
Score: 4 — one light back-reference short of a completely repetition-free transcript.

## responsiveness_and_adaptation

Score 1: Interviewer asks specifically about servicing costs; the candidate's next turn continues discussing CAC and never addresses servicing.
Score: 1 — answers a different question than the one asked.

Score 3: Interviewer redirects the candidate from CAC toward servicing costs; the candidate acknowledges it but only pivots to servicing costs a full turn later.
Score: 3 — adjustment after redirection takes more than one turn.

Score 4: Interviewer redirects toward servicing costs; within the same turn the candidate switches and immediately uses the new detail about installation and maintenance.
Score: 4 — adjusts within the same turn; only a minor lag because the candidate briefly finishes the prior point first.

## groundedness

Score 1: Candidate: "Their CAC is probably around $2,000 and LTV is maybe $15,000" — no such figures appear anywhere in the case data or transcript, and the candidate presents them as known facts.
Score: 1 — states facts not provided in the case and treats them as given.

Score 3: Candidate correctly uses the 18% gross margin and 38-month CAC payback from the case and flags one assumption explicitly ("assuming the software-only model keeps the same growth rate"), but later uses a specific "roughly 25% margin improvement" figure without flagging it as an assumption.
Score: 3 — blurs the line between given data and inference at least once.

Score 4: Every number the candidate uses (18% margin, 38-month payback, 14-month runway, 340 clients) is one already given in the case, and every time the candidate reasons beyond the data ("I'll assume large cooperatives average at least twice the hectares of small ones") it is explicitly flagged as an assumption.
Score: 4 — nearly every claim traceable to a case fact, assumptions clearly labeled.

## confidence_calibration

Score 1: Candidate: "I'm certain the answer is to raise prices" after looking at a single data point, then reverses to "actually maybe it's not pricing at all" as soon as challenged.
Score: 1 — certainty despite thin evidence, no principled correction.

Score 3: Candidate is well-calibrated on the CAC/margin diagnosis but states the final recommendation ("they should definitely exit small cooperatives entirely") more categorically than the case data supports — the data shows small cooperatives are loss-making per account, not that full exit is clearly right.
Score: 3 — at least one conclusion delivered with more certainty than the evidence supports.

Score 4: Candidate hedges appropriately on the software-only timeline ("this could work, but it depends on financing within the 14-month window") while stating the CAC/margin diagnosis firmly, since that part is directly supported by the case numbers.
Score: 4 — confidence matches evidence in nearly every turn.

## multi_turn_coherence

Score 1: Candidate identifies CAC payback as the key issue in an early turn, then several turns later says "actually I think it's really more of a marketing problem" with no acknowledgement of the earlier conclusion.
Score: 1 — a later turn contradicts or ignores an earlier conclusion.

Score 3: Reasoning builds turn over turn overall, but the connection between the segment-profitability point made mid-interview and the final recommendation is never made explicit — the recommendation just appears.
Score: 3 — the thread mostly holds, but a connection to an earlier point is missing at least once.

Score 4: Each new turn opens by tying back to the prior point ("given the CAC payback issue we just covered, the natural next question is...") all the way to the recommendation, with one transition — from the math to the creative-ideas turn — that is not explicitly bridged.
Score: 4 — builds turn over turn with at most one unclear transition.
