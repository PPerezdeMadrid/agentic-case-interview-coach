## case_opening

Score 1: Interviewer gives the case prompt. Candidate: "Okay, so, losses are widening despite 70% ARR growth… right. Let's look at the cost structure then. It sounds like a lot of the costs are probably hidden in delivering the product to each account, because 70% growth is pretty good, so revenue isn't the immediate problem. I think the first step is to break down the unit economics: what does it cost to acquire a customer, and what's the ongoing cost to deliver the product to them?"
Score: 1 — there is no recap of the situation and no clarifying question about the objective anywhere in this turn; the candidate goes straight into hypothesis-driven analysis. A confident, fluent tone is not a recap. Do not credit this turn with "a solid recap" or "asking about the objective" unless you can point to the literal sentence that does it — a rationale that can't quote it is not grounded in the transcript.

Score 2: Candidate: "Okay — ARR's up but losses are wider, got it. I'll start by breaking down where the costs are coming from."
Score: 2 — a thin, perfunctory recap of one fact is present, but there is no clarifying question anywhere in the turn about the objective, timeline, or scope; the candidate moves straight to analysis instead.

Score 3: Candidate: "So if I understand correctly, ARR is growing fast but losses are widening. Before I dive in, is the main objective here to fix profitability, or also to sustain growth? Do we have a target timeline?"
Score: 3 — recap and two relevant clarifying questions are present, but generic; nothing here previews how the candidate plans to structure the case.

Score 4: Candidate: "Let me make sure I've got this right: the client has grown ARR 70% annually for two years but losses have widened each quarter, and runway is limited.
